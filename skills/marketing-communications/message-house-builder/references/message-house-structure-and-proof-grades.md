# Message house structure and proof grades

Used by the message house builder at Procedure steps 4 to 11. Defaults apply only where the inputs are silent; the organisation's own proof standard, claim rules and template override every table here, and the override is noted in the header.

## Anatomy

| Part | What it holds | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Roof: umbrella statement | One sentence: what the subject is, for whom, the one outcome the sources support | Under 25 words; every word rests on a pillar; no superlative unless quoted |
| Pillars: key messages | Two to four, each a headline under twelve words and a one-sentence message | Distinct from each other; each supports the roof; each has proof beneath |
| Foundation: proof points | Two to four per pillar, each traced to a source with a date and a grade | No proof without a source; no source without a date or UNKNOWN |

## Umbrella statement test

| Question | Passes when | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | A noun the sources use for the subject | Open question |
| For whom? | An audience named in the audience material | UNKNOWN in the statement; coverage table cannot close |
| What outcome? | An outcome at least one grade A or B proof supports | Flag "outcome unsupported" |
| Is every pillar behind it? | Each pillar's message contributes to the outcome | Pillar flagged "does not support roof" |
| Any superlative or comparison? | Only where quoted from a source | Flag and move to claim sensitivity |

## Pillar tests

- Distinct: two pillars that could be merged without losing a proof point are merged.
- Single idea: a headline containing "and" is split unless the sources treat the two ideas as one.
- Relevant: the "why this audience cares" line quotes or cites audience material; otherwise UNKNOWN.
- Proven: at least one proof point at grade A or B; otherwise "weak foundation".
- Ordered: pillars follow the order in which the audience material ranks the concerns; with no ranking, source order.

## Proof grades

| Grade | Definition | Examples | What the grade tells the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Measured or verified by a party outside the organisation, document seen, dated | Independent test report, audit, certification document, named customer statement with written consent, published research the agent was given | Candidate for external use; the approver named in the claim sensitivity table or the inputs confirms |
| B | Measured inside the organisation with method and date visible | Usage data export, survey results with sample size and date, internal test report | Candidate for use with the date and method visible; owner confirms |
| C | Asserted inside the organisation without a visible measurement | Product sheet wording, a sales deck claim, an expert's statement in a meeting note | Internal working evidence only; not offered for an external claim until a document raises it |
| D | No source seen, or the source is named but not supplied, or two sources conflict | "Studies show", an award named without the citation, a figure with no origin | Flagged UNSOURCED; not usable until sourced; never silently removed |

Grade rules:
- A grade describes the evidence; it is not a decision that a claim may be published.
- The agent never raises a grade. Raising a grade requires a document the user supplies.
- A grade A or B proof older than the freshness limit is marked "fresh: no" and keeps its grade; the flag asks for a newer reading.
- Shareable externally is read from the source or the constraints (confidential markings, consent notes); absent, UNKNOWN.

## Claim sensitivity

| Claim type | Trigger words or shapes | Substantiation required before external use |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative | better, faster, cheaper, more than, versus, unlike | A grade A comparison against the named alternative, and the naming policy permits it |
| Superlative | best, first, only, leading, largest, number one | A grade A source that states the ranking with its scope and date |
| Financial | saves, return, cost reduction, revenue, percent | Grade A or B figure with method, period and population; finance approver |
| Health | health, wellbeing, clinical, treats, prevents | Regulated wording as the constraints state; legal approver |
| Environmental | green, sustainable, carbon, net zero, recyclable | Grade A measurement and the scope it covers; legal approver |
| Safety | safe, safer, prevents accidents, reduces risk | Quoted only, with its source and scope; safety approver. A message house never states that a product or programme makes an operation, site or task safe, and nothing in it authorises operations, permits, isolations or work |
| Security or privacy | secure, encrypted, compliant, certified | The certificate or audit document with its scope and date; security approver |
| Regulatory or legal | approved, licensed, compliant with | The approval or licence document and its scope |

## Existing messaging comparison

| Verdict | When | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Kept | The line matches a pillar or the umbrella and has grade A or B proof | Cite the proof |
| Kept, flagged | The line has no proof at A or B, or contradicts a source | State the gap or contradiction; the owner decides |
| Changed | The line keeps its meaning but is reworded to match the evidence | Show old and new side by side |
| Dropped | Only when the user says so | The agent proposes; it does not drop |

## Usage notes conventions

- "Say" lines carry the pillar message or a proof point as worded in its source, with the grade in brackets.
- "Do not say" lines list the words from the claim sensitivity table that the proof does not yet support, and any banned term from the constraints.
- Usage notes are guidance for writers, not copy; they never contain a slogan the sources do not state.

## Words that do not appear

Unless quoted from a source with its reference: best, leading, first, only, unique, revolutionary, seamless, world-class, industry standard, proven, guaranteed, trusted by, everyone, always, never, safe, secure, compliant, and any figure without a source and a date.
