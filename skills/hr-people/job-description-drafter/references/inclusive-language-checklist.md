# Inclusive language checklist and default structure

Used by the job description drafter at Procedure steps 4, 5 and 6. Every change made under this checklist is recorded in the change table with original, replacement and reason. The checklist guides wording; it makes no legal determination.

## Requirements

Ask of every essential requirement: could a person do the job as described on day one without it? If the brief does not answer, the item is desirable and goes to the confirmation list.

| Pattern in the brief | Rewrite to | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "N years of experience in X" | A capability statement: what the person has done, at what scale, how many times | Years measure time, not ability; the number excludes people who reached the capability faster or by another route. Keep the original in the confirmation list. |
| "Degree in X required" (brief does not say why) | Desirable, or "qualification or equivalent experience in X" | Keep as essential only where the brief states a regulatory or client requirement, with the source. |
| "Must be able to lift, stand, drive, travel" | The task itself: "the role involves moving stock up to the stated limit", "the role includes visits to sites in ..." | Describes the work, not the body. Flag as a possible genuine occupational requirement for confirmation. |
| "Native speaker", "mother tongue" | "Fluent written and spoken X" or the level the brief names | Fluency is the requirement; origin is not. |
| "Recent graduate", "digital native", "young and dynamic", "mature", "seasoned" | Remove the age marker; keep any capability it stood for | Age is not a requirement. |
| "Culture fit", "fits our culture" | The observable behaviours the team values, named | Fit is unassessable and tends to reproduce the existing team. |
| Long lists of "must have" tools or certifications | Split: which are needed on day one (essential), which can be learned (desirable), which are verified by document rather than assessed | Reduces false essentials. |

## Wording

| Flag | Examples | Replacement approach |
|---|---|---|
| Gendered words and pronouns | he, she, his, her, chairman, manpower, salesman, "guys" | you, they, the role holder, chair, staffing, sales representative, team |
| Coded words | ninja, rockstar, guru, wizard, aggressive, dominant, fearless, competitive, hard-charging, nurturing, supportive, warm | Say what the person does: "leads negotiations to close", "coaches team members", "meets deadlines under load" |
| Ableist phrasing | "hit the ground running", "see the big picture", "stand up in front of", "walk the floor", "blind spot", "crippling" | "be effective in the first weeks", "understand the wider context", "present to", "visit teams", "gap", "severe" |
| Overwork signals | "work hard, play hard", "fast-paced, always on", "whatever it takes", "thrives under pressure" | State real conditions: on-call rota, peak periods, travel days, deadlines. Facts, not attitude. |
| Family and availability | "no commitments outside work", "flexible to work any hours" | The actual pattern, hours and notice arrangements as stated, or `[TBC: working pattern]` |
| Acronyms and internal jargon | Team codes, system names, project names, undefined abbreviations | Spell out on first use; describe the system by function if the name means nothing outside the organisation |
| Superlatives about the employer | "world-class", "best-in-class", "leading" | Cut, or replace with a fact the brief provides |

## Sentence rules

- One idea per bullet. A responsibility is a verb, an object and an outcome: "Prepare the monthly cost report so that budget holders see variances within five working days."
- Requirements start with "You have", "You can" or "You are able to" unless the template sets another voice.
- Readable by someone outside the organisation. If a sentence needs the org chart to be understood, rewrite it.
- No requirement, preference or hint concerning age, sex, gender, race, nationality, religion, disability, marital or family status, or similar characteristics. Anything of that kind in the brief is quoted in the confirmation list under "Refer to the people team or legal" and left out of the draft.

## Default structure

Use only when the user has no template and typed a go-ahead. Label the draft "generic structure, replace with your organisation's template".

1. Role title, team, location and working pattern, reports to, contract type, grade or salary (each `[TBC]` if not given)
2. About the role: purpose in two to four sentences
3. What you will do: six to ten responsibility bullets
4. What you bring: table of requirements, essential and desirable, with source
5. How you will work: relationships, ways of working, conditions (travel, hours, on-call) as stated
6. What we offer: only facts the brief or user provides; otherwise `[TBC: benefits]`
7. Adjustments and equal opportunities statement: `[TBC: organisation's standard wording]`, never drafted by the agent
8. How to apply and closing date: `[TBC]`
