# Briefing and FAQ guide

Used by the policy change briefing skill at Procedure steps 2 to 7. The guide fixes definitions, order and wording. It does not decide policy content and makes no legal determination.

## Change type definitions

| Change type | Test | Goes to the briefing |
|---|---|---|
| Unchanged | Same wording, or differences limited to numbering, formatting or a corrected typo. | Only under "what stays the same". |
| Reworded | Different words, same obligation, entitlement, scope and timing. Both wordings quoted so the reader can check. | Only under "what stays the same". |
| Changed | Any difference in who is covered, what is provided, how much, by when, who approves, who is responsible, or what follows a breach. | Yes, one sentence. |
| Added | A clause with no counterpart in the previous version. | Yes, one sentence. |
| Removed | A clause in the previous version with no counterpart in the new one. | Yes, one sentence; if it removes an entitlement, mark as a reduction. |

When a clause is reworded and the two wordings could be read differently, class it as changed and raise an open question rather than deciding the intent.

## Practical effect categories

| Category | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Who may use or is covered by this clause. |
| Entitlement | What is provided, how much, how often. |
| Process step | What someone has to do, in what order, with which form or system. |
| Deadline | By when a request, notice or decision must happen. |
| Approval route | Who decides or signs, and any escalation. |
| Responsibility | Who is accountable for an action or record. |
| Consequence | What follows a breach, a missed deadline or a refused request. |
| Definition | The meaning of a term the other clauses rely on. |

A single clause may carry several categories. List each on its own line in the change log.

## Employee briefing skeleton

Default budget under 400 words. Second person. Headings exactly as below, in this order.

1. What is changing. One sentence per material change, in plain words, clause reference in brackets. Reductions state what is lost: "From <date>, <entitlement> ends (clause 4.2)."
2. Who this affects. As the policy scopes it. If a change touches a subset, name the subset in the policy's own words.
3. From when. The effective date, and any transition rule for cases already started. `[TBC: effective date]` if no source gives it.
4. What you need to do. Only actions the policy requires of employees, each with its clause. If the policy requires nothing of employees, say "Nothing; the changes apply automatically" only if a clause states so, otherwise write `[TBC: policy owner to confirm whether any action is required of employees]` and add the item to the open questions.
5. What stays the same. Only clauses classed unchanged or reworded. No more than one short paragraph.
6. Where to read more and whom to ask. The full policy's title and version; the contact role the policy names, otherwise `[TBC: contact]`.

## Plain language rules

1. One idea per sentence. Under 25 words where possible.
2. Prefer the policy's own defined terms over synonyms; a synonym can change the meaning.
3. Say who does what: "Your manager approves" rather than "approval is granted".
4. Numbers, dates and limits exactly as the clause writes them, including units and currency.
5. No "simplified", "streamlined", "improved", "modernised", "aligned" or "enhanced" as a description of a change. Describe the change itself.
6. No reasons unless the change note gives them; quote or closely paraphrase and attribute to the policy owner.
7. Acronyms expanded on first use, as the policy expands them.
8. No reference to any named individual or to any individual's circumstances.

## FAQ question triggers

Generate a candidate question for each row that applies, then keep 8 to 15 by dropping duplicates and merging near-duplicates.

| Trigger | Question pattern |
|---|---|
| Material change | "A team member asks me what <change> means for them. What do I say?" |
| Transition rule present or absent | "What happens to a request made before <date> under the old rule?" |
| Manager role in a process step | "What am I required to do when <step> happens, and by when?" |
| Approval route changed | "Who approves <item> now, and do I still need to sign?" |
| Removed entitlement | "How do I respond to someone who expected <removed item>?" |
| Definition changed | "Does <term> still mean the same thing?" |
| Known question supplied | Kept in the words it was asked. |
| Consequence clause | "What does the policy say happens if <breach or missed deadline>?" |

Answer rules: each answer restates the clause and gives its reference; an answer that would need an example, a threshold or an exception the text does not contain is replaced by "Open question N, see list".

## Overstatement words

Search the briefing and FAQ for these words. Keep each only where the clause uses the same or equivalent wording; otherwise reword to the clause's terms or move the point to an open question.

always, never, guaranteed, automatically, entitled, must, all, none, immediately, unlimited, at any time, in every case, without exception.

## Holding lines for managers

Used in the open questions list until the people team settles the item. Pick the pattern that fits and fill it with the clause reference.

- "The policy is silent on this point. I have asked the people team and will come back to you by <date TBC>."
- "Clause <n> is being clarified. Do not act on the change in clause <n>, or on the arrangement it replaces, until the people team confirms which applies. I will come back to you by <date TBC>."
- "This touches your contract or a collective agreement. Please raise it with the people team directly."
- "This is a health and safety procedure. Follow the steps as written in clause <n>; no step is optional unless the clause says so."

A holding line never decides the question, never promises an outcome and never names an individual.
