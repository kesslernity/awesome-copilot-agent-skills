# Question and anchor guide

Used by the interview scorecard builder at Procedure steps 4, 5 and 7. The guide sets the shape of questions and anchors. It does not decide who is hired and makes no legal determination.

## Default rating scale

Used when the organisation gives no scale. Level 3 is the requirement as the job description states it.

| Level | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Little or no evidence | The candidate could not give a relevant example, or the example shows the opposite of the competency. |
| 2 | Partial evidence | A relevant example, but the candidate's own actions, the result or the reasoning are missing or vague after probing. |
| 3 | Meets the requirement | A specific example where the candidate's own actions produced a result at the scale the job description describes. |
| 4 | Exceeds the requirement | As level 3, and the candidate also shows judgement beyond the role (anticipated a problem, changed a method, taught others), with a measured effect. |

## Question rules

1. Job-related. Every question maps to a competency, which maps to the job description. If the map is missing, the question goes.
2. Past behaviour first. "Tell me about a time when you ..." asks for something that happened. Situational questions ("How would you ...") are a supplement, one per competency, built from a stated responsibility.
3. One thing at a time. No double questions. No lists of three things to cover in one answer.
4. Open, not leading. Do not name the answer in the question ("Tell me about a time you showed resilience by staying late" names the answer and rewards overwork).
5. Probes follow a fixed shape: What was the situation and your part in it? What did you do yourself, step by step? What was the result, and how do you know? What would you do differently?
6. No trick questions, brain-teasers, puzzles or "sell me this pen" tasks. They measure practice at interviews, not the job.
7. No hypotheticals about the organisation's own confidential matters, and no request to produce unpaid work in the interview beyond what the planned work sample states.
8. Same core questions for every candidate at the same stage. Probes may vary with the answer; the core does not.
9. Plain language. A candidate outside the organisation must be able to understand the question without the org chart or internal acronyms.

## Prohibited topics

Never written as a question, a probe or a "look for", whatever the source asks. Requests of this kind are listed under "Excluded from assessment, refer to the people team" with the wording quoted.

- Age, date of birth, year of graduation, "how long until you retire".
- Sex, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation.
- Marital or partnership status, pregnancy, children, childcare, family plans.
- Race, ethnicity, national origin, first language, accent, "where are you really from".
- Religion, belief, observance, days off for religious reasons.
- Disability, health, medical history, medication, sick leave record, mental health.
- Trade union membership.
- Anything about a protected characteristic disguised as availability ("can you work every weekend", "any commitments outside work").

Adjustments a candidate may need for the interview are arranged by the organisation before the interview, on the candidate's request. They are not a question in the scorecard and never influence a rating.

## Anchor patterns

An anchor is a sentence an interviewer can compare notes against. Use these patterns and fill them with the competency's content.

| Pattern | Example for a "problem analysis" competency |
|---|---|
| Presence of a specific example | "Describes one specific problem, where it arose and their own role in it." |
| Own action, not team action | "Names the steps they personally took to find the cause, rather than what the team did." |
| Evidence of method | "Explains how they ruled out at least one alternative cause." |
| Result and how it was measured | "States the outcome and the measure that showed it (time saved, defects reduced, cost avoided)." |
| Learning or transfer | "Says what they changed in their approach afterwards, or how they applied it to another case." |
| Absence, for level 1 and 2 | "Speaks in generalities; cannot say what they themselves did; gives no result." |

Do not write: "strong analytical skills", "good communicator", "team player", "passionate", "culture fit", "gut feel says yes". Each of these is an impression, not evidence.

## Time defaults

- A primary past-behaviour question with its probes takes about 8 minutes.
- A situational question takes about 5 minutes.
- Introduction 5 minutes; candidate's questions and close 5 minutes.
- A 60-minute stage therefore carries about six primary questions, or four primary and two situational. If the plan needs more, split across stages or mark questions optional; never cut silently.

## Scoring guidance text

The scorecard reproduces these points in its own words:

- Write evidence notes during the interview; choose the rating afterwards, from the notes.
- Rate independently before the panel discussion.
- In discussion, compare evidence against the anchors, not interviewers' overall impressions.
- The overall view is a pattern across competencies. Do not sum ratings unless the organisation's rule says so; weights are `[TBC]` until confirmed.
- Ratings describe evidence gathered in this interview. They do not certify competence, authorise any work, or decide the appointment; the hiring manager and the people team decide.
