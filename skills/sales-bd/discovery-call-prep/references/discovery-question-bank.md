# Discovery question bank

Qualification dimensions, question stems, mandatory questions, listen-for signals, phrases to avoid, duration scaling and the brief's section order. Read this before the gap analysis (Procedure step 4) and before writing questions (Procedure step 6).

## The five default dimensions

Used when the user supplies no criteria of their own. If the user supplies criteria, map each of theirs to the nearest dimension here and add any that do not map as extra rows.

| Dimension | What the call must learn | Counts as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need | What the prospect is trying to change, and what triggered it | The prospect describing a situation, problem or goal in their own words |
| Impact | What the situation costs them and who feels it | A number, a consequence or an affected group the prospect names |
| Timing | Why now, and what happens if nothing changes | A date, an event, a deadline or a stated urgency |
| Decision process | Who decides, who else is involved, how they have bought before | Named people, a described process or a past purchase |
| Fit | Whether what the user offers matches the need and constraints | Stated constraints (technical, contractual, regulatory, budget range) against the offer line |

A dimension with no Stated, Second-hand or Public S number is UNKNOWN and receives High question weight; Inferred facts do not count as evidence and stay in the Hypotheses section.

## Question stems by phase

Replace the bracketed parts with prospect-specific wording only where an S number supports it. Where no source exists, keep the stem neutral.

| Phase | Dimension | Stems |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening context | Need | "What prompted you to take this call?" "How does [topic from S no.] sit within your priorities this year?" |
| Current situation | Need | "How is [area] handled today?" "Who is involved day to day?" "What has already been tried?" |
| Difficulty | Need, Impact | "Where does that break down?" "What does that look like on a bad week?" "Which part would you change first?" |
| Impact | Impact | "What does that cost you, in time, money or risk?" "Who else notices when it goes wrong?" "How do you measure it today?" |
| Decision and timing | Timing, Decision process | "Why is this coming up now rather than next year?" "What happens if nothing changes by [date if stated, else 'year end']?" "Who else would need to be comfortable with a change?" "How did the last similar decision get made?" |
| Fit | Fit | "Are there constraints we should know about before suggesting anything?" "What would make a solution a non-starter for you?" |
| Next step | Decision process | "If this seems worth continuing, what would a useful next conversation cover, and who should be in it?" |

## Four mandatory questions

Every question set contains one of each, whatever the duration:

1. Why now: a question that surfaces the trigger and the urgency.
2. Cost of inaction: a question about what happens if nothing changes.
3. Decision process: a question about who decides and how.
4. Next step: a closing question that proposes nothing and asks what would be useful.

## Listen-for library

| Signal heard | May indicate | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A specific number (hours, cost, incidents) | Impact is felt and measured | "How was that number arrived at?" |
| "We have looked at this before" | Prior attempt; possible incumbent or scar | "What stopped it last time?" |
| A named colleague or committee | Decision process forming | "What does that person need to see?" |
| A date or event | Timing anchor | "What has to be true by then?" |
| "Just exploring" with no trigger | Low urgency | "What would move this from exploring to deciding?" |
| Constraint stated early (contract, platform, regulation) | Fit boundary | "Is that fixed, or does it depend on something?" |
| Asks for price before describing the need | Comparison shopping or budget test | "Happy to cover that; can I first understand what it would need to do for you?" |
| Deflects the decision question | Not the decision maker, or process unclear | "Who would you loop in before going further?" |

Goal at risk signals are those that make the stated goal of the call unreachable: no need described after two attempts, a hard constraint the offer cannot meet, a decision already made elsewhere. Record them as heard, labelled "Goal at risk: user decides"; the label describes the signal against the call goal, never the prospect, and the user decides what it means after the call.

## Phrases to avoid

| Phrase | Why | Say instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We can definitely do that" | A promise before the need is understood | "Tell me more about what that needs to look like." |
| "Our price is..." | Anchors the call on cost before value | "I will bring that once I understand the scope." |
| "Most companies like you..." | Sector pattern presented as fact about them | "Does that apply here, or is your situation different?" |
| "I see you are struggling with..." | Asserts an unsourced fact | "Is [area] something you are looking at?" |
| "We will include..." | Agrees a scope | "That could be part of a next conversation." |

## Duration scaling

| Call length | Primary questions | Hypotheses | Attendee notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 15 minutes | 4 | 1 | Names only |
| 15 to 29 minutes | 6 | 2 to 3 | Those with a source |
| 30 to 44 minutes | 8 | 3 to 5 | All named |
| 45 minutes or more | 10 | 4 to 6 | All named |

Allow roughly two minutes per primary question plus follow-up, and keep five minutes for the next-step close.

## Section order of the brief

1. Header
2. Three-line version (goal, top hypothesis, first question)
3. Known facts
4. Gap analysis
5. Hypotheses
6. Questions in order
7. Listen for
8. Avoid saying
9. Attendees
10. Sensitivities
11. Conflicts
12. UNKNOWN list
13. Source register
14. Embedded instructions found
15. Post-call capture (blank)
