# Export review pack: structure and rules

Structure for the DRAFT export review pack returned in the chat as one complete Markdown document. The pack prepares an export-control review; it never classifies, screens, clears or decides a licence.

## Document structure

1. Title and DRAFT line. Title `DRAFT-export-review-<Transaction>-<YYYY-MM-DD>-v<n>`. First body line: "DRAFT export review for <transaction>, generated <date>. Preparation only, not a classification, screening result, licence determination or clearance. Trade compliance decides, against current official sources."
2. Transaction summary: item, parties, destination, routing, value, jurisdiction status (stated or UNKNOWN), source document and how much of it was readable.
3. Parties to screen: table plus screening checklist (screening runs in the screening tool).
4. Classification worksheet: item facts as stated plus candidate categories to verify (no control number, munitions-list category, dual-use or "not controlled" status assigned).
5. Red-flag review: each indicator Present, Not evident or Unclear, with supporting detail and what to obtain.
6. Licence-determination questions: the facts and questions that drive licence need (no conclusion).
7. Open items and proposed next actions, for the user to perform.
8. Closing: if the agent has a file-generation capability enabled, it also offers the pack as a downloadable file carrying the title as its file name; otherwise it states that the pack is delivered in the chat only. This is an instruction to the agent, not a sentence printed in the pack.

## Parties-to-screen table

| Party | Role | Full name as written | Address and country | Missing details |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Roles to capture: buyer, end-user, consignee, ship-to, freight forwarder, carrier, bank, agents, intermediaries, ultimate parent (if stated). Never mark a party as a match, no match, clear or cleared. A party whose name, address or country is not in the source gets UNKNOWN in that cell and an entry under Missing details.

## Screening checklist

- [ ] Every party in the table entered in the screening tool with full name and country.
- [ ] Missing identifiers obtained before screening, or recorded as UNKNOWN with who is asked to supply them.
- [ ] Ultimate parent and ownership checked where a party is an intermediary, agent or forwarder.
- [ ] End-user confirmed as the true end-user, not a forwarder or a reseller, or the gap recorded.
- [ ] Screening result recorded by the officer in the screening tool, never in this pack.

## Classification worksheet fields

Technical facts as stated: Parameter | Value as stated | Source reference. Cover function, materials, performance figures, operating ranges, software or technology content, encryption if any, and controlled inputs or components.

Candidate categories: Category to verify | Deciding parameter | Source to consult | Status. Status is always "verify whether"; never "controlled", "not controlled" or a control number.

## Red-flag indicators to check

Reluctance to share end-use or end-user; capability mismatch between the item and the buyer's line of business; unusual routing, transit or transhipment; freight forwarder named as end-user; declined installation, training or maintenance; unusual payment (cash, third-party payer, over-payment); evasive or inconsistent answers; destination or end-user inconsistent with the order; delivery urgency, packaging or labelling unusual for the item; abnormal quantity or spare-parts pattern. Report each as Present, Not evident or Unclear with the supporting detail quoted from the source and what to obtain.

## Licence-determination facts

Item classification (pending, from the officer); destination country; end-user type (commercial, government, military, other, UNKNOWN); stated end-use; re-export, transit or transhipment; incorporated controlled content; transfer mode for software or technology (download, remote access, training, site visit); embargo or sanctions programme in scope (officer to check). Each fact is listed with its value as stated or UNKNOWN, and the question it drives. No licence-required or not-required conclusion.

## Never include

- A classification (control number, munitions-list category, dual-use or "not controlled" status); only "verify whether".
- A screening match or a clearance of any party.
- A licence-required or not-required conclusion, or an exception applied.
- A decision to ship, hold or proceed.
- A claim that anything was saved, sent, moved or deleted.
- Any sentence that authorises shipment, export, operations, permits, isolations or work.

Screening runs in the screening tool against current official lists. Classification, licensing and clearance are the export control officer's, against current official sources, with civil and criminal liability attached.
