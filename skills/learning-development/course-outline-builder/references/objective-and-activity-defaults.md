# Objective and activity defaults for course-outline-builder

Read at steps 3 to 6 and 8 of the procedure. Every default applies to the draft. The owner may overrule any of them; the closing report records each overruled default.

## Objective structure
Verb, object, condition, standard, in that order. Condition names what the learner is given (a form, a screen, a scenario, a tool). Standard names how well, as a source states it (a threshold, a tolerance, a rule reference); no source, UNKNOWN. One performance per objective; "and" in an objective usually means two objectives. Example: "Given a completed timesheet, identify every entry that breaches the overtime rule in policy section 4" (condition: the timesheet; verb: identify; object: breaching entries; standard: the quoted policy section).

## Verb families
- Recall: list, name, state, identify, define, locate, recognise. Learner reproduces or points to something stated.
- Apply: calculate, complete, select, classify, operate, follow, enter, configure, check against. Learner uses a stated rule, step or threshold in a given situation.
- Analyse: compare, distinguish, diagnose, prioritise, trace, detect. Learner combines two or more stated points or chooses which stated rule applies.
- Produce: draft, assemble, prepare, record, present, hand over. Learner creates a work product that can be checked against a source.
- Not allowed as objective verbs: understand, know, appreciate, be aware of, learn, be familiar with, be exposed to. Rewrite each as the observable behaviour the owner would accept as proof.

## Activity types and default timings
Timings are per activity for one cohort at the default session length. Live means facilitated, in person or online; self-paced means the learner works alone.
- Briefing or demonstration: 10 minutes live, 5 minutes self-paced. Facilitator or system shows; learner watches. Not a performing activity.
- Worked example: 10 minutes live, 5 minutes self-paced. Facilitator or system walks through the task with the source open. Not a performing activity.
- Guided practice: 15 minutes live, 10 minutes self-paced. Learner performs the verb with prompts and immediate correction. Performing activity.
- Independent practice or scenario: 20 minutes live, 10 minutes self-paced. Learner performs the verb without prompts on a case drawn from the sources. Performing activity.
- Case discussion: 15 minutes live only. Learners compare or diagnose a case; needs an Analyse objective. Performing activity for Analyse verbs.
- Role play or simulation: 20 minutes live only. Two or more learners enact a stated procedure or conversation. Performing activity.
- On-the-job task with checklist: duration UNKNOWN unless the owner states it; learner performs the verb at work against a checklist drawn from the source. Performing activity; requires a named observer role, which the owner supplies.
- Reflection or debrief: 5 minutes live, 3 minutes self-paced. Learner records what they would do differently. Not a performing activity.

A performing activity is one in which the learner carries out the objective's verb on its object. Every module needs at least one, or the coverage rows of the objectives it serves read thin.

## Module sequencing
- Default: one module per terminal objective, in this order of precedence: prerequisite objectives first, then simple to complex, then the workflow order the sources describe. State which rule set the order in the Ordering note column.
- Merge two objectives into one module only where they share a content source and the same task; say so in the Ordering note. Never merge to fit a duration constraint.
- Each module row carries identifier, title, objectives served, prerequisites, duration estimate with basis, delivery mode and content sources with section reference; a module with no source reads "content source: UNKNOWN".

## Duration basis
Module estimate = sum of its activity timings, plus 10 per cent for transitions in live delivery, plus the assessment timing (see Assessment alignment) if the check runs inside the module. A check with duration UNKNOWN, or one that runs outside the module, is excluded from the sum and named in the closing report. Total = sum of modules. State the basis in the module row; state the constraint and the difference in the closing report. A module over the maximum session length is split into sessions at an activity boundary, never mid-activity.

## Assessment alignment
- Recall verb: knowledge check (multiple choice, matching, short answer), compared against the quoted passage.
- Apply verb: task or scenario with a single correct outcome per the source, compared against the quoted rule or step.
- Analyse verb: case with a written or spoken response, compared against a checklist of the stated points the response must combine.
- Produce verb: work product marked against a checklist drawn from the source; each checklist line cites its passage.
- Default timings, one check for one cohort, unless the owner states a figure: Recall check 10 minutes; Apply task or scenario 15 minutes; Analyse case response 20 minutes; Produce work product 30 minutes where produced in session, UNKNOWN where produced at work or marked afterwards. A check fitting none of these reads UNKNOWN. State the basis (default or owner figure) in the Duration and basis column.
- Every check names what is observed, what it is compared against (quoted or UNKNOWN), when it runs, its duration with basis and its objective. Pass mark, weighting, attempts, remediation and consequence are DECIDE lines for the owner in every case.

## Delivery mode notes
- Self-paced with no facilitator: role play, case discussion and observed on-the-job tasks are not available; the outline lists the objective as thin and proposes a live component as a DECIDE item.
- On the job: every activity needs an observer role and a checklist; both are owner-supplied, else UNKNOWN.
- Blended: each module carries its own mode; the outline states which parts are live and which self-paced.
- Audience languages differ from source language: the outline flags every source passage that would need translation; it translates nothing.

## Restricted words
In objectives, activities, assessments and the outline's own text, these words appear only inside a quotation with its location: competent, qualified, certified, authorised, licensed, permitted, approved, safe, compliant, sufficient, passed, ready for duty, signed off. Completing a course is never written as conferring any of them.
