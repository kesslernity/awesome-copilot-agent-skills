# Needs codebook and anonymisation for training-needs-synthesis

Read at steps 2 to 5, 8 and 10 of the procedure. The organisation's own codebook replaces the default one when supplied; the closing report names which was used.

## Default codebook: training needs
Code a record to a need only where the record's own words name the skill or the difficulty. One record may carry several codes.
- Tool or system operation: using a named application, device or platform.
- Procedure or process: following a stated workflow, form, approval path or handover.
- Product, service or domain knowledge: facts about what the organisation makes, sells or maintains.
- Mandatory or regulatory as stated: a requirement the record attributes to a law, regulator, standard or policy. Coded from the record's attribution only; this agent verifies no obligation.
- Data and analysis: spreadsheets, reporting, reading dashboards, interpreting figures.
- Writing and communication: emails, reports, presentations, meetings, customer or colleague conversations.
- People management and leadership: feedback, delegation, performance conversations, running a team.
- Customer or stakeholder interaction: handling requests, complaints, negotiations.
- Safety practice as stated: a safe-working step the record names. Coded as stated; the report never judges whether current practice is safe.
- Onboarding and role basics: knowledge a new starter lacks about the role itself.
- Other: named in the record but fitting none of the above; candidate for a new code.

## Default codebook: non-training barriers
A record that names a cause outside skill is coded here, never as a need: workload or time, tooling or system defects, access or permissions, unclear process or ownership, staffing, equipment. Each barrier row carries its quote and a suggested owner only where a source names one.

## Evidence types
- Self-reported want: the respondent asks for training or says they would like to learn something.
- Self-reported difficulty: the respondent says they struggle with or cannot do something.
- Manager-observed gap: a manager note describes a skill shortfall seen in work.
- Incident or audit finding: a report, audit or review attributes an event or nonconformance to a skill gap. Coded from the document's attribution only.
- Mandatory as stated: the record says the training is required. No verification here.
A record carries the type its own words show. A survey answer is never coded manager-observed; a manager note is never coded self-reported.

## New codes and unclear records
- A need appearing in three or more records and fitting no code becomes a candidate code, listed in Data quality with its records, not added to the codebook without the owner.
- A record that could fit two codes takes both. A record too vague to code ("more training please") is coded "unspecified need" and counted; it never feeds a specific need's count.
- A record that contradicts itself is coded to each position and listed under Disagreements if the positions concern the same need.

## Anonymisation table
Apply before quoting or counting. Replace, count replacements by type, never list originals.
- Person name: [person]. A named colleague or manager: [colleague, role] where the role is stated, else [colleague].
- Employee number, email, phone: [id].
- Team or unit smaller than the minimum group size: [small team].
- Role whose headcount or record count is below the minimum group size: [small role] in every table and every quote except the Roles register.
- Site, office or location that would identify fewer than the minimum: [site].
- Specific date or shift that pins an event to a person: [date].
- Named incident, case or customer: [incident] or [customer].
- Health, family, disciplinary or grievance detail: not quoted, not coded; the record is noted as containing sensitive personal data.
Re-identification test: could a manager of that role, reading the quote, name the author with reasonable confidence? If yes, paraphrase and tag "[paraphrased]" or drop the quote.

## Quote selection order
Only from a side (survey or manager) whose count for that role meets the minimum group size. Within a need and a role, in record code order: first records that carry both a role and a clear evidence type; then records with a role only; then the rest. Cut with "[...]" to at most 40 words. Never choose by strength of wording, emotion or length. A quote used once is not reused under a second need.

## Counting rules
- n counts records, never people; a respondent with two answers on one need counts once for that need.
- N for a role is its coded survey records and M its coded manager records, always stated separately; n counts against N and m against M, never against the sum.
- Shares appear only where N meets the floor, always beside n. Headcount appears as context, never as a denominator for a training percentage.
- A role, site or team below the minimum group size is suppressed with the complementary cell that would reveal it; survey and manager sides are judged on their own count, and no quote is drawn from a side below the minimum; each suppression is stated.

## Restricted words
In the report's own text these words appear only inside a located quotation from a record: competent, incompetent, qualified, unqualified, certified, authorised, failing, underperforming, weak, poor performer, not fit, ready, sufficient, compliant, safe. The report counts and quotes; it never rates.
